#43 Spatial Rendering of Virtual Wing Sounds Synchronized with Human Flapping Motion and Its Influence on the Sense of Wing Ownership

Nanase Fujiwara, Yoichi Haneda

Previous research in virtual reality has shown that changing the visual appearance of a user’s virtual avatar can alter the user’s own body representation. In contrast, comparatively little is known about whether such transformations can be induced solely through auditory feedback that is spatiotemporally synchronized with voluntary body movements. This study investigates whether a sense of having wings on one’s back—or of one’s arms being wings—can be elicited by rendering spatially dynamic wing sounds that follow the user’s flapping motions. We developed an interactive audio system that employs a 42-channel spherical loudspeaker array to render real-time moving virtual sound sources synchronized with the user’s arm movements tracked via motion capture. Spatial rendering was implemented using Near-Field Compensated Higher-Order Ambisonics (NFC-HOA) with IIR filters. When the user performs horizontal arm opening and closing motions, virtual sound sources are positioned either behind the user or at the arm locations. The wing sounds are synthesized by modulating the gains and spectral characteristics according to arm velocity and acceleration. A subjective evaluation was conducted to assess whether the proposed system could induce a sense of wing ownership. To examine the influence of sound source distance and direction, we compared NFC-HOA rendering with two conditions: Higher-Order Ambisonics (HOA) rendering without distance control, and stereo reproduction using loudspeakers placed either behind or in front of the listener. The results showed that a sense of having wings on the back was rarely induced under any condition when sound sources were rendered behind the listener. In contrast, when sound sources were rendered at the listener’s arm positions, the results suggest that a sense of the arms being wings was induced under both the NFC-HOA and HOA conditions. These findings suggest that presenting spatially localized auditory feedback at the actual position of a moving body part can contribute to inducing altered body-part representation, even in the absence of visual feedback.

#20 Using HRTF Interpolation for Improving Crosstalk Cancellation Algorithm in Binaural Audio Headrest

Nefeli Aikaterini Dourou, Valeria Bruschi, Alessandro Terenzi, Stefania Cecchi

Binaural audio rendering enables immersive spatial sound reproduction by replicating human auditory perception and is increasingly explored in loudspeaker-based systems such as automotive headrests. This paper presents an enhanced crosstalk cancellation approach for binaural audio headrest systems, combining space-averaged filtering with head-related transfer function (HRTF) interpolation. The proposed method addresses practical limitations of measurement procedures by enabling accurate filter design from a reduced and potentially asymmetric set of HRTFs. Interpolation techniques reconstruct a denser spatial sampling, improving robustness across listening positions. Evaluation in terms of channel separation shows that the crosstalk filter derived by interpolated HRTFs enhances performance compared with the filter designed by asymmetric measurements. In contrast, HRTF upsampling did not affect channel separation when the filter was designed from symmetric positions.

#69 Reinterpreting Berio’s Electroacoustic Music in an Immersive-Audio Environment

Francesco Canavese, Francesco Giomi, Giovanni Magaglio, Francesco Vogli

The interpretation of fixed-media electroacoustic music involves processes of historical revision, technical updating, and perceptual reformulation. Based on the spatial reinterpretation of Luciano Berio’s Thema (Omaggio a Joyce) and Visage, this paper examines the potential of immersive audio for reworking stereophonic works. The aim is not simply to enlarge the listening field, but to render explicit spatial tensions and directionalities arguably embedded in the sonic material, yet constrained by the original stereo format. The methodology combined comparative listening of restored versions and previously realized multichannel interpretations with detailed analysis of the source material and the design of spatialization strategies based on differentiated-order Ambisonics encoding, combining low- and high-order representations. A key stage involved the use of source-separation and spectral-unmixing tools to isolate signal components and assign them to distinct spatial positions or trajectories. The case study suggests that enveloping audio can operate not only as a means of spectacularizing space, but as a critical tool for reactivating the complexity of a work. Spatialization thus emerges as a form of critical reading of the electroacoustic repertoire, where restoration, spectral separation, and spatial distribution converge to produce a new perceptual ecology of listening.

#41 From Fragmentary Evidence to Interactive Auralization: Acoustic Inference and Preliminary Interfaces for the Coricancha

Luna Valentin, Alba Menéndez Pereda

Heritage sites frequently exist as a collection of fragmentary material and documentary evidence, limiting the possibility of reconstructing accurately and comprehensively their original acoustic conditions. This challenge is evident at the sacred center of Coricancha (Cuzco, Peru), where extensive colonial-period and modern transformations obscure the spatial configuration and architectural makeup of the complex as it stood when it was built during the Inca Empire (\textit{ca}. 1440–1532 C.E.). While these alterations pose an obstacle to acoustic research, they do not prevent it altogether. This paper addresses how, under conditions of restricted access and partial data, acoustic measurements can support bounded interpretations of past sonic environments and how these interpretations can be meaningfully communicated. The approach adopted is grounded in \textit{in situ} impulse response measurements collected across accessible areas of the site. Acoustic parameters, including sound pressure level, clarity coefficient, and frequency response, are analyzed to characterize patterns of audibility, intelligibility, and spatial differentiation. Rather than attempting a speculative sonic reconstruction of Coricancha, these measurements are treated as empirical constraints that reveal zones of acoustic continuity and separation, offering insight into how the imperial Inca built environment as it was developed at this sacred center may have structured the contemporary sonic experience. To extend these findings beyond static analysis, we develop a minimal web-based interface that enables interactive exploration of the measured acoustic field that can be used by specialists and the public at large.

#33 Resonances of Stones: Immersive Architectural and Acoustic Reconstruction for Acoustic Heritage

Gianluca Aresu, Alessandra Montali

This abstract presents Resonances of Stones, an immersive architectural and acoustic reconstruction of the Bazeos Tower in Naxos developed as a case study in acoustic heritage. The project combines in situ First-Order Ambisonics impulse-response capture, acoustic analysis, binaural auralization, and interactive audio-visual XR deployment in Unity and Wwise. RTPC-driven interpolation between zone-based impulse responses, (convolution reverb) and dynamic early reflections support a 6DoF listening experience. The heritage dimension is grounded in a musicological framework that interprets the tower as a stratified cultural space and links its historical, spiritual, and social identities to the selection and reinterpretation of Greco-Byzantine poetic-musical sources. The paper proposes a method connecting immersive audio technologies, musicological interpretation, and participatory heritage reactivation for contemporary and future audiences.

#35 WAVES-4K: A 6th-Order Ambisonic and Synchronised 4K Video Dataset of Natural Inland and Coastal Waterside Soundscapes

Craig Cieciura, Philip Jackson

Natural soundscapes present unique challenges for spatial audio capture, rendering, and machine-learning applications due to the interplay of spatially extended stochastic textures and highly directional transients. Humans are sensitive to the spatial fidelity of these environments; yet, existing datasets typically offer lower-order Ambisonic or monophonic audio paired with video. This paper introduces the Waterside Audio-Visual Environments (WAVES-4K) dataset, a multimodal dataset comprising two hours and forty-two minutes of synchronised 6th-order Ambisonic audio and 4K video. Utilising a 64-channel spherical microphone array (Eigenmike EM64) and a Lumix GH5 or iPhone 15 Pro, we captured 17 audio-visual perspectives at 14 sites across three distinct UK topographies, each with natural water sources: the Lake District (characterised by rivers, lakes, and avian woodland calls), the Cornwall coastline (broadband sea, beach and river mouth textures), and the University of Surrey lakeside during the dawn transition. While the captures prioritised natural environments, the inclusion of intermittent human-origin sounds provides further data for research into robust sound event detection (SED) and source separation. The dataset is highly suited for benchmarking immersive rendering techniques, conducting psychoacoustic studies of nature-based presence, and developing multimodal machine learning models. We detail the field-recording method and provide an inventory of the captured scenes.

#40 A non-invasive additive manufacturing framework for interactive 3D replicas in cultural heritage interpretation.

Pedro-Pablo Chambi-Condori, Melina Zegarra

The preservation and dissemination of cultural heritage are increasingly based on non-invasive digital technologies to avoid the risks of manipulation, environmental damage, and limited access to fragile objects. Three-dimensional (3D) digitization and additive manufacturing allow for the production of exact physical replicas, but most current applications only generate static reproductions with limited interpretive capabilities. This work experimentally proposes a non-invasive additive manufacturing framework for 3D replicas capable of interacting with them and providing synchronized audiovisual feedback. The application is carried out on the petroglyph resources of Miculla rock art in the Tacna Region, located in southern Peru. The study's workflow integrates the digital capture, physical fabrication, and sensory processes into a workflow that preserves the integrity of the original pieces. The results of the experimental evaluation demonstrate that the produced replicas maintain high morphological fidelity and allow for reliable interaction, low-latency audiovisual synchronization, and greater user participation. The results indicate that interactive replicas can serve as living interpretive interfaces, a scalable and conservation-focused solution for disseminating, educating, and making heritage accessible.

#17 Validating a Head-Tracking Spatial Beamformer in Virtual Reality: A Comparative Study of Simulated, Software-Based, and Real-World Hearing Aid Beamforming

Giso Grimm, Volker Hohmann

Using simulated, artifact-free spatial filtering in an interactive, immersive virtual acoustic reality enables the spatial and spectral effects of signal enhancement to be disentangled when analyzing human behavior. This study validates a fully simulated beamformer within the interactive virtual acoustic rendering system TASCAR. We compared its performance with that of a minimum variance distortionless response (MVDR) beamformer with sampled microphone signals, and an MVDR beamformer with simulated microphone signals, both implemented in the openMHA framework. The fully simulated beamformer dynamically steers its direction in real time based on the participant’s head orientation. It applies broadband attenuation depending on the angular distance between the head direction and the direction from which sound sources arrive. Image sources are treated the same as direct sources with the correct image source position to ensure consistent spatial filtering across the acoustic scene. In the rendering system, diffuse sound fields are simulated using first-order Ambisonics. This requires a dedicated spatial filtering approach based on modal beamforming in the proposed filter. Performance was assessed via beam pattern analysis and diffuse sound field attenuation across identical acoustic scenarios. The results revealed systematic differences in directional selectivity and diffuse sound field attenuation between the fully simulated and the MVDR beamformers, which can be attributed to the frequency dependency of the MVDR beamformer.

#19 Reducing The Geometric Complexity of Rooms For Simpler Acoustic Simulation

Omar Ahmed, David De Roure

Acoustic simulation provides a promising avenue to facilitate a software approach that closely resembles the real-life task of arranging instruments and microphones in a room. However, simulations typically take a long time, especially on consumer-grade hardware. The objective of this work is to use deep learning to reduce the geometric complexity of rooms, represented as point clouds, to alleviate the computational load of acoustic simulation without sacrificing the perceptual accuracy of the output. We examine an existing approach of reconstructing point clouds with an autoencoder and adjust the model architecture to improve results for indoor spaces. We devise an autoencoder capable of smoothing room geometries and provide the novel loss function required to do this; crucially, our autoencoder incorporates a novel acoustic loss term which ensures that perceptually important features are undisturbed, whilst perceptually irrelevant features are smoothed. We investigate the relationship between smoothing and acoustic integrity by adjusting importance hyperparameters. We find that our model smoothes perceptually irrelevant features and retains acoustic integrity, producing an average sound ray direction discrepancy that reaches $6.470^\circ$; this value indicates perceptual success. Further, we investigate the wider applicability of our model by applying our model to the task of acoustic panel placement and assessing our method in the context of digitised historical spaces.

#27 A Web-Based Framework for Virtual Acoustic Space Rendering and Collaborative Studio Evaluation

Victoria Keddie, Frank Zimmer, Gerriet Sharma, Cobi van Tonder

Collaborative work across distributed studios raises a persistent challenge: how can practitioners sense a remote space’s acoustic character before visiting it? Digital twins require reconstruction and simulation; simple documentation fails to convey spatial experience. Neither serves a consortium of labs seeking to share and compare their spaces. This demo presents a browser-based platform for navigable virtual acoustic environments, developed by Fränk Zimmer and Sounding Future with the Spatial Sound Consortium, founded and directed by Victoria Keddie. Built on Three.js and ATMOKY’s binaural audio engine, it proposes a middle path: the virtual build, conveying acoustic impression and visual orientation without full simulation. Browser delivery removes installation barriers, letting a studio in Chicago and a lab in Belfast inhabit the same space.

Labs provide impulse responses measured at predefined positions under a standardized protocol; convolution applies each space’s acoustic fingerprint. Visuals range from .glb geometry or 360° photography to Gaussian splatting. Multiple users navigate a shared environment in first- or third-person view, each hearing it differently. The tool makes this asymmetry compositionally productive, reflecting the Consortium’s view of spatial audio as relational. Member studios, from multichannel arrays to compact binaural rooms, act as pilot sites and co-creators. They shape the platform through iterative feedback and listening sessions around a standardized test piece. Key challenges include latency, consistent binaural rendering across headphones, and an accessible interface.

Early outcomes include multi-user sessions and position-based IR interpolation. It sits within a wider ecosystem — a global studio map, nine member sites, and a travelling performance program — that no existing platform was designed to hold.

#79 Numerical Assessment of the Impact of Extensive Heat Pump Deployment on Urban Soundscapes through Representative Case Studies

Giacomo Salvadori, Francesco Leccese, Fabio Bisegna, Maurizio Pieve, Raniero Trinchieri

The progressive electrification of residential heating and cooling systems is expected to significantly increase the diffusion of air-source heat pumps within urban environments. Although heat pumps contribute to energy decarbonization objectives, the widespread installation of outdoor units may produce non-negligible impacts on the urban acoustic environment. This study presents a numerical investigation aimed at quantifying the effects of extensive heat pump deployment on urban soundscapes under different urban morphologies and installation configurations. Advanced outdoor sound propagation simulations were performed using the Wölfel IMMI 2023 software platform. Four representative Italian urban districts were selected as case studies, characterized by different building densities, spatial distributions, and building heights. The analysed urban grids covered areas of approximately 75,000 m² and included residential contexts ranging from low-density suburban districts to compact high-density urban fabrics. For each building, geometric and volumetric properties, number of housing units, and estimated thermal demand were determined through GIS-based reconstruction methods using GEOscopio and Google Earth data. Heat pump thermal capacities were assigned proportionally to residential volumes, while sound power spectra were derived from experimentally characterized units grouped by thermal capacity ranges. A total of 24 simulation scenarios were developed by combining four urban layouts with six installation configurations. The analysed configurations included both decentralized systems, with outdoor units associated with individual apartments, and centralized systems with rooftop or ground-level installations. Simulations assumed continuous full-load operation, omnidirectional point-source radiation, reflective building façades, and receiver heights representative of pedestrian exposure conditions. The results demonstrate that both urban morphology and installation strategy strongly influence the resulting acoustic impact. Dense urban districts characterized by narrow street canyons and high building concentrations exhibited the highest spatial extent of elevated sound pressure levels. Decentralized configurations with multiple façade-mounted units generated widespread medium-to-high noise levels, whereas centralized rooftop solutions generally reduced pedestrian-level exposure despite localized increases near buildings. In several scenarios, areas exposed to sound pressure levels above 50 dB(A) occupied a significant fraction of the modelled urban grid, particularly in compact urban fabrics and centralized ground-level configurations. The study highlights the importance of integrating acoustic criteria into future urban energy transition strategies. The proposed methodology provides a useful framework for evaluating the acoustic sustainability of large-scale heat pump deployment and supports the development of urban planning, architectural, and plant-design guidelines aimed at mitigating adverse effects on urban soundscapes.

This paper synthesises three studies on the creation, propagation and interpretation of sound. It first examines Luigi Russolo’s Futurist philosophy and the intonarumori, mechanical instruments that brought industrial and urban noise into musical expression.
