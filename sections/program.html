Keynote Speakers

Angela Bellia
University of Palermo, Italy
Angela Bellia is Full Professor of Musicology and Music History in the Department of Cultures and Societies at the University of Palermo. She earned her MA and PhD at the University of Bologna and completed her classic piano and opera singer studies at the Italian Conservatories of Music. Her research interests span sonic heritage, soundscape archaeology, sound studies, heritage acoustics, computational archaeomusicology, and digital heritage.

Her research has been conducted at prestigious institutions, including the Institute of Fine Arts (New York University), the École des Hautes Etudes en Sciences Sociales (Paris), the University of Zürich, and the National Research Council of Italy (CNR). Her research activities have been supported by the European Union program Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA).

She has been the Principal Investigator of the AURAL project and is the recipient of a recently awarded Advanced Grant from the Italian Science Fund (FIS) for the VOICING project, funded by the Ministry of University and Research (MUR). Angela Bellia has been invited or selected to present at over 150 peer-reviewed international conferences and advanced schools across multiple continents.

She serves as Editor in Chief of the TELESTES series and of Telestes: An International Journal of Archaeomusicology and Archaeology of Sound. As the Chair and founding member of the Archaeomusicology Interest Group (AMIG) of the AIA, she has organized numerous international workshops and colloquia for their annual meetings. She is the author of numerous books and contributions published by prestigious academic publishers, including Bloomsbury, Brill, Fabrizio Serra, Franz Steiner Verlag, Oxbow, and Routledge.

Her contributions to promoting European research have been recognized with the MCAA "Outstanding Contributor Award" (2021).
Emma-Kate Matthews
University College London (UCL)
Dr Emma-Kate Matthews (b. 1986) is an architect, composer, musician, digital artist and researcher working at the intersection of sonic and spatial practice. Her research centres on what she terms "spatiosonic practice": the poetic and practical reciprocities between making sound and making space, explored through site-specific, spatialised audio-visual works, custom instrument design, and architectural research. She completed her PhD at the Bartlett School of Architecture, UCL in 2025 and is currently Associate Professor and Director of Fabrication and Design Collaboration there, teaching on the MSci Architecture and MEng Architecture and Engineering programmes. She is lead editor of The Routledge Companion to the Sound of Space (Routledge, 2024), co-organised the CMMR 2025 conference with Yamaha and the V&A East Storehouse, and hosts a monthly radio show on RTM.FM.

Her compositions have been performed internationally at venues including the Basílica de la Sagrada Família, the Southbank Centre, the Barbican, Brighton Festival, and most recently at URANIA in Zagreb, where Betwixt & Between received its world premiere performed by Ansambl Synchronos in 2025. She composed for the London Symphony Orchestra's Panufnik Composers' Scheme and has released electronic-classical works as a solo artist on labels including Algebra, NMC and Accidental Records. Her music has been broadcast on BBC Radio 3, Resonance FM and Tokyo Radio.

Under the series Resonant Bodies, she designs and fabricates her own instruments using advanced digital modelling and manufacturing methods. Her work has been nominated for the Lumen Prize and the Aesthetica Art Prize, and exhibited internationally at the Royal Danish Academy, the Royal Institute of British Architects, York Art Gallery and Make Southwest. She founded her transdisciplinary practice EKM Works in 2012.

Her research has been published in Nature HSSComms, Drawing: Research, Theory, Practice, and Organised Sound, and she has been profiled by the World Economic Forum, BBC Classical Music Magazine and Clot Magazine. She received Highly Commended honours at the Sound of the Year Awards in both 2022 and 2023. Her audiovisual work ‘Chroma Zone’ was recently exhibited at Heni gallery and she has talks scheduled at London's V&A and ROCA galleries.

See: https://ekm.works for more.
David Monacchi
Ecoacoustics • Composer • Interdisciplinary Artist
David Monacchi (Italy, 1970) is an ecoacoustics researcher, composer and interdisciplinary artist. He has been developing the project Fragments of Extinction for 25 years, conducting field research in the world's remaining areas of undisturbed primary equatorial forest in all continents. The recipient of multiple international awards for his work on intangible heritage of ecosystems, Monacchi is pioneering a cross-disciplinary approach based on unique 3D soundscape recordings, to raise awareness on the biodiversity crisis through science-based sound art.

His Eco-acoustic Theatre's patented invention (a spherical amphitheatre dedicated to sensorial and cognitive deep listening experiences) is being built in museums and public spaces, now traveling as SONOSFERA®. Monacchi has been portrayed by journals as Nature and PNAS, and is now full-professor at the State Conservatory of Pesaro, Italy. Collaborations with IUCN and ICCROM, to promote advocacy in primary forest soundscape conservation, culminated in an invited talk for the plenary session of the UN COP-14 Biodiversity Conference and for UN COP-27 Climate Change Conference.

https://www.fragmentsofextinction.org/
Tin Oberman
University College London (UCL)
Tin Oberman is a researcher, urban planner, architect, and musician. He holds an M.Arch. and PhD in Architecture and Urbanism from the University of Zagreb and has held research and teaching positions in Croatia and the UK. Currently based at University College London, his work explores soundscape research, spatial planning, and the relationship between sound, architecture, and urban environments.
Deep Sen
Apple, Inc.
Deep Sen received the B.E. and Ph.D. degrees from the University of New South Wales, Sydney, Australia, in 1990 and 1994, respectively. Since 2019 he has been leading the development of Audio Codecs and Immersive Audio technologies at Apple, Inc. Preceding that, he was a Senior Director with Qualcomm Technologies, Inc. At Qualcomm he led the development of the MPEG-H immersive audio suite of technologies and its standardization at various application standards such as ATSC 3.0 and DVB.

From 1994 to 2003, he was with the Speech and Audio Laboratories, AT&T Bell Laboratories, NJ, USA, and from 2003 to 2011, he was a Faculty Member with the School of Electrical Engineering, University of New South Wales, Australia. He has multiple peer-reviewed publications and multiple granted patents. His past experience includes speech and audio coding, psychoacoustics, bio-mechanical modeling of the cochlea, blind source separation, beamforming, and hearing prosthetics.

Dr. Sen is a member of the Acoustical Society of America, Audio Engineering Society, and an Elected Member of the IEEE Speech and Language Technical Committee.
